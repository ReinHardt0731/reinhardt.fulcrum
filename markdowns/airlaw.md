# International Airlaw
## Conventions

### Freedoms of the Air

| Freedom | Type | Description | Example |
| ------- | ---- | ----------- | ------- |
| 1st | Technical | Fly over a foreign country without landing | Manila to Tokyo, overflying Taiwan |
| 2nd | Technical | Land in a foreign country for non-traffic purposes (refueling, maintenance) | Stopover in Guam to refuel |
| 3rd | Commercial | Carry passengers or cargo from your home country to a foreign country | Philippines to Singapore |
| 4th | Commercial | Carry passengers or cargo from a foreign country to your home country | Singapore to Philippines |
| 5th | Commercial | Carry traffic between two foreign countries on a flight to or from your home country | Philippine carrier flying Manila to Tokyo to Los Angeles, selling Tokyo–LA seats |
| 6th | Commercial (unofficial) | Carry traffic between two foreign countries via your home country | Passengers from Singapore to Tokyo via Manila |
| 7th | Commercial (unofficial) | Operate entirely between two foreign countries, with no stop in your home country | Philippine carrier flying Singapore to Tokyo |
| 8th | Commercial (unofficial) | Consecutive cabotage: carry traffic between two points within a foreign country, on a flight that began in your home country | Philippine carrier flying Manila to Tokyo to Osaka, selling Tokyo–Osaka seats |
| 9th | Commercial (unofficial) | Stand-alone cabotage: operate domestic flights within a foreign country | Philippine carrier flying Tokyo to Osaka only |

- **Memory tip:** the 1st and 2nd freedoms are *technical* (no traffic is carried), and the 3rd to 9th are *commercial*.
- **Official status:** only the 1st to 5th appear in international agreements (the 1st and 2nd in the International Air Services Transit Agreement, the 3rd to 5th in the International Air Transport Agreement). The 6th to 9th are unofficial and come from industry practice.

### Sovereignty, Navigation and Regulation

| Year | Convention / Treaty | Key Point |
| ---- | ------------------- | --------- |
| 1919 | [Paris Convention](https://www.icao.int/milestones-international-civil-aviation) | Recognized each state's complete and exclusive sovereignty over its airspace and established the International Commission for Air Navigation (ICAN). |
| 1944 | [Chicago Convention](https://www.icao.int/zh-hans/publications/doc-series/convention-international-civil-aviation-doc-7300) | Foundation of modern civil aviation; established ICAO, provides for Annexes containing Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs), and states the principle of sovereignty in Article 1. Signed in 1944; entered into force in 1947. |
| 1944 | [International Air Services Transit Agreement](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | The “Two Freedoms Agreement,” covering the first and second freedoms of the air. |
| 1944 | [International Air Transport Agreement](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | The “Five Freedoms Agreement,” covering the first through fifth freedoms of the air. |

### Liability and Carriage by Air

| Year | Convention / Protocol | Key Point |
| ---- | --------------------- | --------- |
| 1929 | [Warsaw Convention](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/postalhistory/the_warsaw_system_on_air_carriers_liability.htm) | Established common rules for international air-carrier liability, including carriage documents and liability limits. |
| 1955 | [Hague Protocol](https://www.icao.int/milestones-international-civil-aviation) | Amended the Warsaw Convention, including by raising passenger liability limits and simplifying carriage-document requirements. |
| 1961 | [Guadalajara Convention](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Extended Warsaw-system liability rules to the actual carrier when it performs carriage for the contracting carrier. |
| 1971 | [Guatemala City Protocol](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Proposed strict liability for passenger death or injury and baggage loss or damage; never entered into force. |
| 1975 | [Montreal Additional Protocols Nos. 1–4](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Replaced gold-franc liability limits with Special Drawing Rights (SDR) in the Warsaw-system instruments. Additional Protocol No. 3 never entered into force. |
| 1999 | [Montreal Convention](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/secretariat/legal/Administrative%20Packages/mtl99_en.pdf) | Modernized and consolidated the Warsaw system. For passenger death or injury it introduced a two-tier system: strict liability up to a threshold, with further liability above it subject to the Convention's defenses. Entered into force in 2003. |

### Surface Damage and Aircraft Rights

| Year | Convention | Key Point |
| ---- | ---------- | --------- |
| 1933 | [Rome Convention](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/postalhistory/the_rome_convention_and_its_modernization.htm) | Addressed damage caused by foreign aircraft to third parties on the surface. The 1952 Rome Convention replaced it. |
| 1948 | [Geneva Convention](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Provides for international recognition of rights in aircraft, including ownership and mortgages. |
| 1952 | [Rome Convention (revised)](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Updated the rules on damage caused by foreign aircraft to third parties on the surface; entered into force in 1958. |
| 2001 | [Cape Town Convention and Aircraft Protocol](https://www.icao.int/cape-town-convention-and-protocol) | Establishes an international-interest regime for mobile equipment, including aircraft objects; the Convention and Aircraft Protocol entered into force in 2006. |
| 2009 | [General Risks Convention](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Convention on compensation for damage caused by aircraft to third parties; not in force. |
| 2009 | [Unlawful Interference Compensation Convention](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Convention on compensation for damage resulting from unlawful interference involving aircraft; not in force. |

### Aviation Security (Unlawful Acts)

| Year | Convention / Protocol | Key Point |
| ---- | --------------------- | --------- |
| 1963 | [Tokyo Convention](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/postalhistory/legal_instruments_related_to_aviation_security.htm) | Covers offences and certain other acts on board aircraft, including the aircraft commander's powers. |
| 1970 | [Hague Convention](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/postalhistory/legal_instruments_related_to_aviation_security.htm) | Addresses unlawful seizure (hijacking) of aircraft. |
| 1971 | [Montreal Convention](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/postalhistory/legal_instruments_related_to_aviation_security.htm) | Addresses unlawful acts against the safety of civil aviation. |
| 1988 | [Montreal Protocol](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/postalhistory/legal_instruments_related_to_aviation_security.htm) | Extends the 1971 Montreal Convention to acts of violence at airports serving international civil aviation. |
| 1991 | [Montreal Convention on Plastic Explosives](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Requires marking plastic explosives for detection. |
| 2010 | [Beijing Convention](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) and [Beijing Protocol](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Updated the Montreal Convention of 1971 and the Hague Convention of 1970, respectively. The Protocol entered into force on 1 January 2018; the Convention entered into force on 1 July 2018. |
| 2014 | [Montreal Protocol](https://www.icao.int/secretariat/legal/current-list-of-parties) | Amends the Tokyo Convention to address unruly passengers, including by expanding jurisdiction to the State of landing and the State of the operator; entered into force in 2020. |

## PCAR

The PCAR is organized into **Parts**, not chapters, and CAAP now publishes it as the "Civil Aviation Regulations (CAR)".

| Part | Title |
| ---- | ----- |
| 1 | General Policies, Procedures and Definitions |
| 2 | Personnel Licensing |
| 3 | Approved Training Organizations |
| 4 | Aircraft Registration and Marking |
| 5 | Airworthiness |
| 6 | Approved Maintenance Organizations |
| 7 | Instruments and Equipment |
| 8 | Operations |
| 9 | Air Operator Certification and Administration |
| 10 | Foreign Air Operators |
| 11 | Aerial Work |
| 13 | Accident and Incident Investigation |
| 18 | Safe Transportation of Dangerous Goods by Air |

- CAAP's advisory circular listing names Parts 2 through 8, 11, 13 and 18 with these titles.
- Parts 9 and 10 are the least certain entries. Part 9 is amended alongside Part 5 in a 2026 proposal covering the operator's maintenance program and MEL, and Part 10 is the regulation tied to foreign air operator approvals.
- Parts 12, 14–17 and 19 onward are omitted because their titles could not be verified. Check the current list on the CAAP website, since CAAP keeps issuing amendments, such as proposals for Parts 1, 2, 7, 8 and 9.
