## Testing and Inspection | Corrosion Prevention and Control

---

### Types of Corrosion

| Corrosion Type | Primary Cause | Well Known For | Key Characteristics | Common Aircraft Locations |
|----------------|---------------|----------------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| **Uniform (Surface)** | General atmospheric exposure (oxygen + moisture) | Most Common | Even metal loss over the entire surface | Aluminum skins, steel structures |
| **Galvanic (Dissimilar Metal)** | Electrical contact between dissimilar metals in the presence of an electrolyte | Two Dissimilar Metals | More active metal corrodes first | Steel fasteners in aluminum, magnesium assemblies |
| **Pitting** | Breakdown of the protective oxide film, often by chlorides or salt contamination | Small Deep Holes | Localized attack that penetrates deeply | Aluminum skins, magnesium components |
| **Crevice (Concentration Cell)** | Trapped moisture and oxygen concentration differences | Hidden Areas | Corrosion confined to tight spaces | Lap joints, under washers, gaskets, rivet heads |
| **Intergranular** | Improper heat treatment or sensitization causing attack along grain boundaries | Grain Boundary Attack | Internal attack between metal grains | Heat-treated aluminum alloys (2XXX, 7XXX) |
| **Exfoliation** | Advanced intergranular corrosion in rolled or extruded metals | Layers Peeling | Surface lifts into thin flakes or layers | Extruded aluminum spars, stringers, longerons |
| **Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC)** | Sustained tensile stress combined with a corrosive environment | Cracks Under Stress | Fine cracks that propagate rapidly | Landing gear, highly stressed fittings, 7XXX alloys |
| **Fretting** | Repeated vibration or slight movement between tightly fitted surfaces | Vibration Wear | Oxidized wear debris at contact surfaces | Riveted joints, bolted joints, bearings |
| **Filiform** | Moisture penetration beneath damaged or poorly adhered paint | Under Paint | Thread-like corrosion beneath coatings | Painted aluminum skins |
| **Microbiologically Influenced Corrosion (MIC)** | Bacteria, fungi, or microorganisms in the presence of water | Biological Corrosion | Localized corrosion caused by microbial activity | Fuel tanks, bilges, water systems |

### Corrosion Recognition Guide

| Observation | Think... |
|-------------|----------|
| Even rust or corrosion over the entire surface | Uniform Corrosion |
| Two different metals touching with corrosion around the joint | Galvanic Corrosion |
| Tiny deep holes | Pitting Corrosion |
| Corrosion hidden under lap joints or washers | Crevice Corrosion |
| Grain boundaries attacked after improper heat treatment | Intergranular Corrosion |
| Layers lifting like pages of a book | Exfoliation Corrosion |
| Fine cracks in a highly stressed part | Stress Corrosion Cracking |
| Black or reddish powder between rubbing parts | Fretting Corrosion |
| Thread-like lines beneath paint | Filiform Corrosion |
| Corrosion associated with bacteria or stagnant water | MIC |

### Notes

- **Sub-chapters:** each chapter is divided into **System–Subsystem–Unit** (e.g., **ATA 32-41-00** is Landing Gear → Nose Gear → Steering).
- **Unused numbers:** gaps (e.g., 13–17, 19, 40, 43, 48, 58–59, 69, 86–90) are reserved or unassigned.
- **Memory tip:** 21–49 is mostly systems, 51–57 is structures, 60s is rotors/props, and 70s is engines.
- **Newer standards:** ATA 100 has been superseded by **iSpec 2200** and, in many places, **S1000D**, but the chapter numbering is still the same.

Chapter 92 and a few of the newer numbers vary by manufacturer, so check the specific aircraft's maintenance manual if you need exact usage. If you're reviewing for your board exam, I can also make a quiz or flashcards for these.

### 6. Temper Basics

#### Process Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Solution heat treatment** | Heats the metal until alloying elements dissolve into the aluminum, then quenches it (cools it quickly) |
| **Natural aging** | Metal hardens by itself at room temperature over time |
| **Artificial aging** | Metal is heated at a moderate temperature to accelerate precipitation hardening |
| **Overaging** | Aging past peak strength for greater stability and corrosion resistance, with reduced strength |
| **Cold work** | Shapes or deforms metal at room temperature, strengthening it |
| **Stabilizing** | Treatment that keeps properties from changing over time |
| **Annealing** | Heating and slowly cooling to soften metal |

#### Basic Temper Letters

| Letter | Meaning | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **F** | As fabricated | No control over properties |
| **O** | Annealed | Softest, most ductile condition |
| **H** | Strain hardened | Non-heat-treatable alloys (1XXX, 3XXX, 5XXX) |
| **W** | Solution heat treated, unstable | Properties change at room temperature |
| **T** | Thermally treated to a stable temper | Heat-treatable alloys (2XXX, 6XXX, 7XXX) |

- **H** is for alloys that get stronger by cold work only.
- **T** is for alloys that get stronger by heat treatment.

### 7. H-Tempers

*Non-heat-treatable wrought alloys are strengthened by strain hardening (cold work).*

#### HXX Format

| Position | Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **First X** (secondary treatment) | 1 | Cold worked only (no anneal) |
|  | 2 | Cold worked + partial anneal |
|  | 3 | Cold worked + stabilized |
|  | 4 | Cold worked + baked |
| **Second X** (degree of strain hardening) | 2 | ¼ hard |
|  | 4 | ½ hard |
|  | 6 | ¾ hard |
|  | 8 | Fully hard |
|  | 9 | Extra hard |

- **Example:** **H14** = cold worked only, ½ hard.

### 8. T-Tempers (Current Standard)

The current T-temper standard (ANSI H35.1) describes each temper by the exact processing steps used.

#### T1 to T4: Natural Aging

| Temper | Process | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Cooled from an elevated-temperature shaping process and naturally aged | Made hot (for example, extruded), cooled, then left to age at room temperature |
| **T2** | Cooled from an elevated-temperature shaping process, cold worked, and naturally aged | Same as T1, with cold work added before aging |
| **T3** | Solution heat treated, cold worked, and naturally aged | Heat treated, then cold worked, then aged at room temperature |
| **T4** | Solution heat treated and naturally aged | Same as T3, without the cold work |

#### T5 to T10: Artificial Aging

| Temper | Process | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **T5** | Cooled from an elevated-temperature shaping process and artificially aged | Made hot, cooled, then heated to age (no solution treatment) |
| **T6** | Solution heat treated and artificially aged | Heat treated, quenched, then aged in an oven (common examples: 6061-T6 and 7075-T6) |
| **T7** | Solution heat treated and artificially overaged | Aged past peak strength for stability and corrosion resistance |
| **T8** | Solution heat treated, cold worked, and artificially aged | Cold work happens before artificial aging |
| **T9** | Solution heat treated, artificially aged, and cold worked | Cold work happens after artificial aging |
| **T10** | Cooled from an elevated-temperature shaping process, cold worked, and artificially aged | Like T5, with cold work added before aging |

#### Memory Patterns

- **T1 to T4** use natural aging; **T5 to T10** use artificial aging.
- **T1, T2, T5, and T10** start from a hot shaping process, with no solution heat treatment.
- **T3, T4, T6, T7, T8, and T9** start with solution heat treatment.
- **T8 vs. T9:** cold work occurs before aging in T8 and after aging in T9.
- **T3 vs. T4:** T3 includes cold work; T4 does not.

### 9. Temper Designation (Older Simplified Version)

This older, simplified version is found in FAA-style handbooks. It uses the same letters with shorter, looser wording; some tempers (T2, T5, T7, and T10) are described differently from the current standard.

| Temper | What it says | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **F** | As fabricated | No special control |
| **O** | Annealed | Softened |
| **H** | Strain hardened | Strengthened by cold work |
| **H1** | Strain hardened only | Cold worked, no anneal |
| **H2** | Strain hardened and partially annealed | Over-hardened, then softened back a bit |
| **H3** | Strain hardened and stabilized | Cold worked, then stabilized |
| **W** | Solution heat treatment, unstable temper | Properties still changing |
| **T** | Treated to produce stable tempers other than F, O, and H | General heading for all T tempers |
| **T2** | Annealed (cast products only) | Used for cast parts, not wrought |
| **T3** | Solution heat treated and then cold worked | Natural aging is implied |
| **T4** | Solution heat treated | Natural aging is implied |
| **T5** | Artificially aged only | No solution heat treatment |
| **T6** | Solution heat treated and then artificially aged | Same as the current standard |
| **T7** | Solution heat treated and then stabilized | Similar idea to overaging |
| **T8** | Solution heat treated, cold worked, artificially aged | Same as the current standard |
| **T9** | Solution heat treated, artificially aged, cold worked | Same as the current standard |
| **T10** | Artificially aged, cold worked | Simplified wording |

### 10. Side-by-Side: T-Tempers vs. Temper Designation
